## Editorial standard

- Canonical lesson Arabic: Fuṣḥā / Modern Standard Arabic.
- Qur'anic quotations: preserve Qur'anic wording and identify them as Qur'anic; do not silently modernise them.
- Vocabulary headwords: fully vowelled citation form, without inventing a sentence-level case ending.
- Sentences: standard spelling and enough vowel marks to remove beginner-level ambiguity. Full case endings should only be added when they have been checked in context.
- Transliteration: one consistent scholarly-friendly system (`ā`, `ī`, `ū`, `ḥ`, `kh`, `dh`, `ʿ`, `ʾ`, etc.). A final short `u` must not be written as long `oo`.
- Present-tense entries such as `يَكْتُبُ` are labelled “he writes,” not the English gerund “writing.”
- Every source item remains traceable to a PDF page.
- Overlap with Level 1 is labelled **Level 2 revision**. It is not removed.

### Review labels

- **Confirmed** - the source is readable and the standard form is clear.
- **Normalised** - the intended item is clear, but spelling, harakat, wording, translation, or labelling has been standardised.
- **Needs confirmation** - the source or the intended classroom pairing is genuinely ambiguous.

### `هَذَا` and `هَذِهِ` (pages 1-2)

The grammar distinction is sound:

- `هَذَا` - this (masculine singular)
- `هَذِهِ` - this (feminine singular)

**Correction:** `حَقِيبَة` is feminine, so the source line should be `هَذِهِ حَقِيبَة`, not `هَذَا حَقِيبَة`.

The remaining page 1 `هَذَا` examples are masculine. The page 2 examples (`سَبُّورَة`, `مِسْطَرَة`, `مَدْرَسَة`, `سَاعَة`, `خَرِيطَة`, `مِرْوَحَة`, `مَكْتَبَة`, `مِبْرَاة`, `مِمْحَاة`, `مِمْسَحَة`, `نَافِذَة`) are feminine.

### `مَنْ` and `مَا` (page 2)

- `مَنْ` asks “who?” about a person.
- `مَا` asks “what?” about a non-person.
- The demonstrative still agrees with the noun: `مَنْ هَذَا؟`, `مَنْ هَذِهِ؟`, `مَا هَذَا؟`, `مَا هَذِهِ؟`.

## 2. Shopping passage (page 3)

### Instruction

`اِقْرَأِ الْفِقْرَةَ وَأَجِبْ عَنِ الْأَسْئِلَةِ الْآتِيَةِ.`

Read the paragraph and answer the following questions.

**Normalised:** the source transliteration’s `ana` is intended to be `ʿan` (`عَنْ`).

### Provisional standard Arabic reconstruction

`يَتَسَوَّقُ أَحْمَدُ يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ. يَأْخُذُ أَحْمَدُ النُّقُودَ مِنْ أَبِيهِ، وَيَذْهَبُ إِلَى السُّوقِ التِّجَارِيِّ. يَشْتَرِي فَاكِهَةً وَخَضْرَوَاتٍ وَلُحُومًا وَبُقُولِيَّاتٍ وَأَدَوَاتِ نَظَافَةٍ وَأَدَوَاتٍ مَكْتَبِيَّةً وَمَلَابِسَ. يَعُودُ أَحْمَدُ إِلَى الْبَيْتِ، وَيَجْلِسُ مَعَ أَبِيهِ، وَيَحْسِبُ كَمْ دَفَعَ مِنَ النُّقُودِ لِلْمُشْتَرَيَاتِ، وَيُعْطِي الْبَاقِيَ لِأَبِيهِ.`

Provisional English:

> Ahmed shops on Friday. He takes money from his father and goes to the market. He buys fruit, vegetables, meat, legumes, cleaning supplies, stationery, and clothes. Ahmed returns home and sits with his father. He calculates how much of the money he spent on the purchases and gives the remainder to his father.

Review notes:

- **Normalised:** `لِأَبِيهِ` is more idiomatic here than the source’s literal `إِلَى أَبِيهِ`.
- **Normalised:** English “stationary” should be “stationery.”
- **Confirmed from the PDF:** the class specifically uses `السُّوق التِّجَارِيّ`; retain it and explain that ordinary `السُّوق` is also sufficient when the distinction is unnecessary.

## 4. Matching exercise (page 4)

The source contains ten left-hand fragments, ten right-hand fragments, and crossing hand-drawn lines. A higher-resolution inspection gives these intended pairs:

- `أَلْوَان` + `الْأَزْرَقُ وَالْأَسْوَدُ وَالْأَحْمَرُ`
- `يُصَحِّحُ الْوَاجِبَاتِ` + `الْأُسْتَاذُ`
- `تَفَضَّلْ` + `أَبِي` -> likely `يَا أَبِي، تَفَضَّلْ`
- `لِلَّهِ` + `الْحَمْدُ` -> `الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ`
- `لَا إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللهُ` + `أَفْضَلُ قَوْلٍ` (wording needs a final grammar check)
- `التِّجَارِيّ` + `السُّوق` -> `السُّوقُ التِّجَارِيُّ`
- `الرَّحْمَانِيَّة` + `الْمَدْرَسَة` appears to form a school name: `الْمَدْرَسَةُ الرَّحْمَانِيَّةُ`
- `الْبَقَاءُ` + `لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى` -> `الْبَقَاءُ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى`
- `مُحَاسِب` + `أَخِي` -> `أَخِي مُحَاسِب`
- `اُدْخُلْ` + `تَفَضَّلْ` -> `تَفَضَّلْ، اُدْخُلْ`

The source includes both a left-hand `تَفَضَّلْ` and a right-hand `تَفَضَّلْ`. Higher-resolution line tracing supports the pairings above. The reconstructed sentence adds vocative `يَا` to produce natural Fuṣḥā: `يَا أَبِي، تَفَضَّلْ`.

## 7. Masculine and feminine grammar (page 12)

Suggested heading:

`النَّحْوُ: الْمُذَكَّرُ وَالْمُؤَنَّثُ`

Core point in the source:

`تَأْنِيثُ الِاسْمِ بِالتَّاءِ الْمَرْبُوطَةِ (ة)`

The noun can often be made feminine with tāʾ marbūṭah, but the learner explanation must say **often**, not “always.” Examples in the source: `سَبُّورَة`, `مَكْتَبَة`, `خَرِيطَة`. The note that the page 2 `هَذِهِ` list is feminine should be retained.

## 10. Verb-family labels (pages 16-20)

Use the definite grammatical labels on the website:

- `الْمَاضِي` - past
- `الْمُضَارِع` - present / imperfect
- `الْأَمْر` - command / imperative
- `الْمَصْدَر` - verbal noun
- `صِيغَة أُخْرَى` - related/other form

“Other form” is intentionally flexible. In this source it sometimes means an active participle and sometimes a related noun; it is not one uniform conjugation slot.

## 11. Verification decisions

The second pass resolved the earlier questions as follows:

1. Retain `السُّوق التِّجَارِيّ`; it is explicit and repeated in the source.
2. Retain curriculum `فَاكِهِيّ`, but show dictionary-preferred `فَاكِهَانِيّ` as an alternative. Retain `خُضَرِيّ`.
3. Add standard examples of legumes, clearly marked as editorial examples rather than handwritten source content.
4. Use stationery examples already present elsewhere in this PDF.
5. Retain the traced matching pairs and add `يَا` to the father vocative for a natural sentence.
6. Retain `فِي السَّقْفِ` and teach `مِرْوَحَةُ السَّقْفِ` alongside it.
7. Retain the angels sentence as a grammar example, not as scripture.
8. Preserve the “first act of worship” line only in the audit; replace it in learner content with the safe sentence `الْقِرَاءَةُ وَالْكِتَابَةُ مُهِمَّتَانِ`.
9. Treat the final writing-family line as two sentences.
10. Translate `جَيِّد` as “good,” not “very good.”
11. Add `احْتِرَامًا` to the two teacher-standing examples so their intended meaning is explicit.

There are now no source-reading questions blocking content modelling. Any future teacher clarification can still replace an editorial example without changing the Level 2 structure.
